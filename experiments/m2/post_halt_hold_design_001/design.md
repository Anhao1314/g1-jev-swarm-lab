## Question and why the historical result cannot answer it

The question is whether a G1 that *first* meets the existing `HALT_SUCCEEDED` gate after a strict task failure remains in a bounded physical state while the same controller continues receiving zero velocity. This is a study of sustained holding, not a new recovery mode or a claim about hardware emergency stopping.

`StopSkill` sends `(0,0,0)` through the official controller and returns on the first complete 500-step (1 s) mean planar speed at or below 0.1 m/s. `LiveMissionSession.run_failure_halt` then checks skill status, final instantaneous speed, duration, displacement, finite/standing/fall and returns; it does not hold. M2.4 Seen and Turn45 crossed with mean speeds 0.0999695815 and 0.0998160573 m/s, margins 0.0000304185 and 0.0001839427 m/s. Their qualifying windows still had 262/500 and 251/500 instantaneous samples above 0.1 m/s. Both are valid historical Halt passes, but each has **zero** postqualification hold steps. The later new-mission trajectories change the command; the refusal arm's stale-state tick is one step and not a hold. Neither supplies the missing evidence. These facts come from the published [first-crossing analysis](../failure_coverage_mechanism_001/halt_analysis.json), which binds the original `parent_result.json` bytes in the M2.4 raw seal.

The controller retains recurrent policy memory, action, target and decimation counter across Stop steps. `StopSkill` itself does not reset it. An ordinary new skill may reset memory, so a new Stop node would answer a different question. The future adapter must extend the exact terminal Halt/Stop state in the same live session and with the same simulator, controller and policy objects, without redispatch or reset. A new session initialized from copied qpos/qvel would not preserve hidden state or contacts and is inadmissible.

## One 2.000-second intervention

At the exact eligible terminal state, apply 1,000 additional native 0.002 s steps through the existing Stop torque computation with command exactly `(0,0,0)`. The 2 s window gives two new, nonoverlapping 1 s speed summaries after the qualifying window and spans 2.5 nominal 0.8 s gait periods; it is long enough to expose recrossing or drift without scanning durations. One window only is tested. The terminal 500 post-step speeds of the original Stop/Halt seed a rolling deque. After **each** of the 1,000 hold steps, append the new speed and score the complete 500-step rolling mean. The normal control seeds its own terminal Stop window in the same way.

`HOLD_SUCCEEDED_BOUNDED_IN_THIS_STATE` requires all 1,000 ensuing rolling means ≤0.1 m/s, hold-final instantaneous speed ≤0.1 m/s, total two-second XY *path length* ≤0.20 m, and finite/standing/no-fall state at every hold step. The 0.1 speed and standing gates are inherited from the existing Halt contract; **0.20 m is a separately frozen new study-specific hold-path bound**, motivated by 0.1 m/s × 2 s, and does not revise the historical 0.5 m post-block Halt displacement gate. Discrete XY path and sampled speed integration need not be identical, so the path gate is not treated as a mathematical consequence of the speed gate. Peak instantaneous speed has no new pass threshold; it is reported because normal gait may exceed 0.1 instantaneously while a complete 1 s mean remains below 0.1. Also report both hold-only disjoint 1 s means, every rolling margin/recrossing, XY net/max displacement, height/tilt, action/target/counter, contacts and warnings when available.

If the rolling mean, endpoint speed or path bound fails, retain the full fixed two-second trace unless a fall, nonfinite/nonstanding state or simulation fault requires early termination. Such a cell is `HOLD_FAILED`; a missing required trace is `INCONCLUSIVE_MISSING_EVIDENCE`, never a pass. Distinguish parent strict PASS with no Halt trigger from parent strict FAIL with no Halt request. Failed Halt and failed ordinary control are separate again; none enter hold. An integrity, reset, command-continuity or hard-budget violation aborts the campaign as `PARTIAL_STOPPED`, preserving all completed and partial raw rows. Ordinary scientific failure does not prompt a repeat or replacement.

## Continuity and raw evidence requirements

Before the first hold step, capture and compare the terminal Stop/Halt state to the hold entry: simulation time, full qpos/qvel/ctrl, applied force, controller action/target/counter, and object identities. Instrument zero post-initialization simulator reset calls and zero controller `reset()` / policy `reset_memory()` calls **from the qualifying Stop step through hold completion**, including the gap before the first hold step. Require zero Mission/Task Graph dispatches and every hold command exactly zero. Journal one raw row for every native step and preserve the unmodified original parent outcome and Halt/Stop receipt separately. Record the source commit, exact protocol and adapter SHA, runtime config, asset/checkpoint and dependency identities, cell/seed, start/end timing, step counts, and any partial/error path. Observers must be shown read-only/equivalent in an offline fake session before physics. Hidden recurrent bytes are not currently snapshotted: same object plus no reset is a continuity witness, **not** a byte-level proof of LSTM identity.

## Budget, decision and audit

The M2.4 refusal runs have 7,140 and 8,267 native steps including one separate stale-state tick; therefore their pre-hold portions are 7,139 and 8,266 steps. Normal control used 5,991. With a fixed 1,000-step hold, expected totals are 8,139, 9,266 and 6,991. Frozen **hard caps** are 10,000 / 12,000 / 8,000 native steps, 30,000 total, 120 s wall per cell and 360 s overall, one attempt each. A watchdog must enforce the caps even if the policy call stalls; it must save an append-only partial receipt. These are planned limits, not permission to run.

The independent postrun auditor must verify source/asset/dependency/protocol hashes, original parent/Halt scores against the published rules, full terminal-to-hold state continuity, zero command/reset/dispatch, exact 1,000 rows or explicit early-stop reason, timing/step budgets, and recompute every rolling mean, path, posture, endpoint and per-cell result from raw rows. Compare repeated construction against the historical prefix, but a mismatch is recorded as a reproducibility/integrity problem, not silently repaired. It must retain negative and missing cells and distinguish physical failure from technical censoring. Two bounded failure-Halt passes would support only these two seen seed-0 states. A negative result in either is a genuine bounded counterexample; missing cells leave the question inconclusive. The normal Stop checks the recording path and context sensitivity. No three-cell result can establish general cross-state reliability or production safety.
